# texture
Bet sizing in `botDecide` is a pure deterministic function of `pot` with no random, style, street, texture or opponent-count term (six formulas total, one of them a literal constant in the most common spot), and the highest-frequency decision at the table — postflop call/fold — contains no `Math.random()` at all, so the observable output is one open size, two postflop sizes, and a fold/call boundary that is identical across all five "personalities."
- ONLY TWO RANDOM DRAWS EXIST IN THE ENTIRE POLICY. `poker-trainer.html:718-823` (botDecide, 106 lines) contains exactly two `Math.random()` calls: line 725 `const roll = Math.random();` and line 744 (the limp roll). Every other branch compares against that single reused `roll`. Neither draw touches a bet size. All variance the judges could possibly observe comes from one uniform per decision, spent entirely on choosing a branch.
- PREFLOP OPEN SIZE IS A LITERAL CONSTANT. Line 737: `const size = Math.min(myStack, Math.max(6, Math.round(pot*1.1)));`. At the first-in spot `pot` is always exactly SB+BB=3 (`newHand`, lines ~995-999), so `Math.round(3*1.1)=3` and the `Math.max(6, …)` floor wins every time. One limper (pot 5) also yields 6. Verified against the judged transcripts: `exp/out/feel-blocks/block-{E,F,G,H}.txt` show 23, 26, 25, 20 opens and every single one is `raises to 6`; the only other values are short-stack shoves from `Math.min(myStack, …)`. The pot-relative expression is decorative — it is unreachable in the spot it governs.
- THE MIN-RAISE LADDER IS PRODUCED BY A SECOND, INDEPENDENT COLLAPSE IN `applyAction`. Line 1099: `target = Math.max(target, S.currentBet + S.minRaise);`. Preflop 3-bets ask for `Math.round(pot*1.0)` (lines 761, 770) = round(3+6)=9, but the legal minimum is `currentBet 6 + minRaise 4 = 10`, so the request is discarded and every 3-bet snaps to exactly 10 — the legal minimum, not a chosen size. `raises to 10` is the only 3-bet number in the coded blocks. Bots therefore min-raise not by policy but because policy sizes fall below legality and get clamped.
- POSTFLOP HAS EXACTLY TWO SIZES AND THE SIZE NAMES THE HAND. Lines 780-781 precompute `halfPot = round(pot*0.5)` and `fullPot = round(pot*0.9)`; line 793 picks between them with `bucket==='strong'?fullPot:halfPot` — a deterministic branch on `s>0.82` (line 779). Raises are `round(pot*0.9)+toCall` (807) or `round(pot*0.8)+toCall` (812). That is the complete sizing vocabulary: six expressions, all pure functions of `pot` and `toCall`. Nothing in `BOT_STYLES` (lines 831-843) is a sizing dial — `{open, bet, fold, limp}` gate frequencies only — so the maniac and the nit bet the identical number of chips into the identical pot. There is no jitter, no street term, no board-texture term, no stack-depth term, no opponent-count term.
- THREE OF FIVE BOTS ARE STRUCTURALLY INCAPABLE OF ENTERING A POT WITHOUT RAISING. The unraised-pot path is: open on `roll<f` (735-740), else the limp branch (742-748) which requires `limpFreq>0 && pct <= thr*2.2`, else the unconditional `return {action: toCall>0?'fold':'check'}` at line 749. `BOT_STYLES` gives `limp:0.00` to nit and selective, `0.05` to solid — so nit and selective literally cannot limp, and solid does so 5% of a narrow band. The judged blocks contain 2-8 limps per 30 hands across five bots (≈150 preflop spots). That is the mechanical origin of "27 hands of perfect raise-or-fold."
- FACING A PREFLOP RAISE, THE FOLD IS A HARD RULE WITH NO ROLL. Line 772 is a terminal `return {action:'fold', …}` reached whenever `pct > defend*1.9` and the `pct <= thr*1.3` bluff-3bet band misses — no `Math.random()` guards it. With `defend = thr*0.55` (754), a nit UTG (`thr = 16*0.70 = 11.2`) folds every hand outside roughly the top 12% with probability 1, except a 3.8% bluff sliver. Continues are confined to three narrow percentile bands, so the loose-call texture a human shows simply has no code path.
- THE MOST VISIBLE DECISION AT THE TABLE IS FULLY DETERMINISTIC. Postflop facing a bet, after the strong-raise (805-810) and draw-bluff-raise (811-813) branches, the call/fold resolves at lines 816-822 by the step function `effective > need + edgeNeed` — zero randomness. Same hole cards, same board, same pot odds ⇒ same answer, always. ARCHITECTURE.md §3.4 concedes "some postflop call/fold branches are threshold-based"; in fact it is all of them. This is precisely why the panel saw no bad calls and no bad folds: the code cannot produce one.
- THE FIVE PERSONALITIES SHARE ONE CALLING BOUNDARY. `edgeNeed = clamp(0.02 * style.fold / nudge.callMult / posCall, 0.005, 0.08)` (line 816). Computed across all five styles the values are nit 0.0270, solid 0.0200, selective 0.0180, station 0.0144, maniac 0.0140 — a 1.3-percentage-point spread in required equity, and the [0.005, 0.08] clamp never binds. `chaseMult` (787) differentiates them only when holding a draw, because it multiplies the `(flushDraw?0.13:0)+(oesd?0.10:0)` term at line 804 and nothing else. In no-draw spots — the majority — the calling station and the nit call and fold in the same places.
- FREQUENCIES ARE BIMODAL, NOT SPREAD. `openFreq` (515-521) returns exactly 1.0 for `pct <= 0.65*threshold`, so most opened hands are opened deterministically; the mixed taper only lives in `(0.65thr, 1.18thr]`. Postflop the effective bet frequencies are strong=0.63-0.95 and weak=0.10-0.20 after `style.bet * posBet * multiway` and `clampFreq`. `clampFreq`'s [0.05, 0.95] floor/ceiling is doing most of the "mixing" at the poles, and a 5-10% event is expected roughly once per 30-hand block — statistically invisible. The result reads as strong→big bet, weak→check, with a thin middle.
- NOTHING CAN DRIFT, BY CONSTRUCTION. `style` is assigned once at line 936 (`style:i===0?null:BOT_STYLES[i-1]`) and is never reassigned anywhere in the file (grep for `.style =` returns no writes). The ctx built at lines 1153-1162 carries no hand number, no session history, no record of the bot's own recent results or recent actions; `myStack` is the only cross-hand quantity and it appears solely inside `Math.min(myStack, …)` as a size cap. The single adaptive channel, `facingReads`, is `null` unless `toCall>0 && aggRoster` (line 1159) — every first-to-act decision is memoryless.
- THE ONE ADAPTIVE CHANNEL IS SUB-PERCEPTUAL WITHIN A 30-HAND BLOCK. `facingNudge` (684-710) computes `callMult = 1 + c*(s.vpip - 0.28)*0.5` where `c = opps/(opps+20)` and `s.vpip` is itself shrunk toward the 0.28 prior by `READS_PRIOR = 20`. A genuinely 50%-VPIP villain observed over 30 opportunities reads as 0.412 with c=0.6, giving callMult = 1.040 — a 4% frequency change on a frequency that is often already clamped at 0.95 or 0.05. `raiseMult` is bounded similarly. Double shrinkage (prior on the rate, confidence on the nudge) mathematically guarantees no visible adjustment on the timescale the judges watched.
- BOT BEHAVIOR IS INDEPENDENT OF HOW MANY PLAYERS ARE IN THE POT. Line 785's `multiway = streetBets >= 2 ? 0.85 : 1` keys off the number of bets this street, not the number of live opponents — the variable is misnamed. Correspondingly line 776 calls `strengthVsRandom(myCards, board, 150)`, which is `equity(mine, board, [{cap:100, aggr:0}])` — strength against exactly one random hand, whether five players or one are still in. Every bot uses the identical strength function; the only per-bot differentiation postflop is three scalar multipliers applied to four hard-coded bucket frequencies.

Tells 1 and 2 are not tuning problems, they are structural absences. Sizing has no stochastic or per-persona input anywhere in the file, so no amount of frequency tuning can produce sizing variety; the fix has to introduce a sizing dimension (per-style sizing tendencies plus per-decision jitter) and remove the two collapse points — the `Math.max(6, …)` floor at line 737 that makes opens constant, and the reliance on `applyAction`'s legal-minimum clamp at line 1099 that turns 3-bets into min-raises. Over-discipline has two distinct roots that need separate treatment: the unconditional folds at lines 749 and 772 (no roll at all) and the deterministic pot-odds threshold at 816-822 (the highest-frequency decision, and the one where humans leak most). Personality separation is currently ~1.3 points of required equity; any humanness work that does not widen `edgeNeed`/calling behavior per style will keep producing one voice. Arc requires a new input: ctx carries no time, no history, no self-knowledge, and `style` is immutable — there is nothing present that could drift.

# voice
Every bot reason in the app is one of 18 template literals hard-coded into botDecide's return statements, with zero persona-conditional text, an RNG value printed verbatim in 73% of them, and a 75-word total English vocabulary across 753 sampled reasons — so all five personas are literally the same speaker reading different numbers.
- ALL bot reason text is 18 hard-coded template literals inside botDecide, poker-trainer.html lines 739, 746, 750, 762, 763, 766, 767, 769, 771, 772, 795-797 (two `why` variants), 799, 808, 809, 813, 819, 822. There is no reason-building function, no phrase table, no variation layer — the string is welded to the return statement of each decision branch, so branch count == phrasing count by construction.
- Measured on the 753 coded-arm reasons in exp/out/feel-blocks/block-{E,F,G,H}.txt: 41 surface skeletons that collapse to those 18 sources; the top 3 cover 400/753 (53%) and the top 6 cover 508/753 (67%). The single line at 750 ('X is a top-N% hand; POS opens ~T%. Opens it F% of the time — roll R said fold.') is 208 of 753 reasons (28%). Total distinct English word types across all 753 reasons: ~75 (104 tokens including hand codes).
- Persona-conditional text is exactly zero. botDecide destructures style at lines 720-721 for four numeric dials only (open/bet/fold/limp); `style.tag` and `style.blurb` ARE present in ctx (passed whole at line 1161) and are never referenced anywhere in the function. Persona therefore reaches the prose only as a rounded percentage inside a shared sentence, and rounding erases even that — nit and maniac both emit 'Opens it 0% of the time'. 12 of the ~24 skeletons each bot uses are shared by all five bots; the rest differ only by which branch the dice hit.
- Raw internal state leaks into prose in three distinct ways: (a) the RNG itself — `const roll = Math.random()` (line 725) is printed verbatim in 552/753 reasons (73%) as 'roll 0.90 said fold'; (b) code-structure names — 'flat-call branch' (763) and 'the slow branch' (809), 15 instances; (c) 190 reasons (25%) narrate a coin flip for a branch whose frequency is 0%, which is arithmetic nonsense as human speech.
- The read-counter leak is `facingNudge`'s `note` field, poker-trainer.html lines 698-709: hard thresholds on shrunk rates emit a fixed 7-word vocabulary (loose/tight/folds often/sticky/3-bets light/overfolds c-bets/calls c-bets) joined into the frozen sentence ' Faces a X aggressor.' It is appended to 10 of the 18 templates and appears in 193/753 reasons (26%), always in identical wording regardless of sample size — `sampleTier` (line 638) and `s.conf` gate the UI dossier but never the reason text, so a 5-opportunity read and a 200-opportunity read speak with identical certainty.
- Register is impersonal-third-person throughout: 0 of 753 reasons contain 'I', 'my', or 'me'. The bots do not speak as players, they narrate as a stats engine ('Opens it 9% of the time', 'checks 84% here'), and 744/753 (99%) contain a '%' sign. `logReason` (lines 1452-1456) prints the bare string with no speaker attribution in the live log; only the export at line 1547 prefixes a name.
- When `toCall === 0` there is no `facingReads` (built conditionally at line 1159), so `nudge.note` is empty and the check/bet templates at 797 and 799 carry no opponent reference at all. Combined with the numbers above, this means 103 of 753 reasons are pure self-talk about a strength percentage, and only 26% of all reasons mention another human being in any form.
- AVAILABLE at reason-build time but unused in prose: `style.tag`/`style.blurb` (persona identity, in ctx, never read); `s.conf` / `sampleTier` (read strength/confidence — computed in shrinkReads line 651, used only by UI); the 13 raw counters in `facingReads` beyond the 5 thresholded ones; `pot` and `myStack` in chips (0/753 reasons mention chips or stack depth); `toCall` in chips; `streetBets` (multiway); `aggressorHadInitiative` (moves callMult at line 696, never surfaces as text); the actual board cards and street name (1/753 reasons mentions any board feature); `dr.flushDraw`/`dr.oesd` outside the single semi-bluff branch (2/753); `tableSize`.
- NOT available at all — no ctx field exists for it, so mood/history-flavoured prose would require a ctx change and a mandatory t3.js re-run per ARCHITECTURE.md §3.1: this bot's own recent results or hands-since-last-playable (boredom/tilt), its own earlier actions this hand or this street, the aggressor's name or seat, any non-aggressor's reads, hand number or session arc. `roster[].reads` is per-seat and persists, and `S.decisions` (line 1126) already accumulates every bot's reason with name/street/action — the history exists in module state but is deliberately walled off from the pure function.
- The reads-to-prose asymmetry is structural: the UI has a richer read vocabulary than the bots do. `readLabel` (lines 654-681) produces confidence-tiered, sample-gated labels for the dossier and export, and `readsLiveLine` (855-869) prints tiers — but the bots' own mouths are limited to `facingNudge`'s 7 tags. The same shared reads model that ARCHITECTURE.md §12 cites as the anti-theatre guarantee is expressed to the player through two completely different text paths, only one of which sounds like it belongs to a person.

The voice problem is not tuning, it is architecture: reason strings are inlined at each `return` in botDecide, so phrasing count is pinned to branch count and any variation has to fight the control flow that produces it. Persona identity (`style.tag`/`blurb`) already rides in ctx unused, and confidence (`s.conf`, `sampleTier`) is already computed — so persona voice and hedged read-talk are reachable without touching ctx or re-running t3.js. Anything mood-, arc-, or history-flavoured is not reachable that way: no such field exists in ctx, and adding one triggers the §3.1 fairness re-verification. The RNG leak (`roll ${roll.toFixed(2)}`), the branch names ('flat-call branch', 'the slow branch'), and the 0%-frequency coin-flip narration are pure text defects with no behavioural coupling at all.

# arc
Bots do adapt, but the whole adaptation channel is a ±3-9% multiplicative tweak to two frequencies, applied only against the current street aggressor, gated behind a prior of 20 and a 30-hand label threshold — so a 30-hand transcript is by construction the warm-up window where nothing can yet show, and no other session-scale state (stack depth, hand index, results, mood) reaches botDecide at all.
- THE ONLY ADAPTATION PATH IS `facingNudge` (poker-trainer.html:684-710), AND IT IS SUB-PERCEPTUAL. It returns exactly two scalars, `callMult` and `raiseMult`, consumed at lines 755-758, 806, 811, 816. I computed the realized magnitudes with the file's own constants after 30 hands of plausible counts: vs a maniac (vpipOpps 30, vpip 17, foldToBet 6/22, threeBet 2/7) callMult = 1.094, raiseMult = 0.969; vs a nit callMult = 0.997, raiseMult = 1.034; vs a station callMult = 1.065, raiseMult = 0.962. Concretely, `defendCall` (line 756, base 0.72) moves from 0.719 at hand 1 to 0.770 at hand 30 against the loosest opponent at the table, and `threeBet` (line 755, base 0.62) from 0.620 to 0.589. A bot faces maybe 5-10 defend-vs-open spots in 30 hands, so the expected number of decisions that actually flip because of 30 hands of reading is well under one. Frequency adaptation that never changes an action is invisible by definition — a judge sees the realization, not the distribution.
- THE PRIOR THAT PREVENTS FAKE CERTAINTY IS THE SAME THING THAT PREVENTS AN ARC. `READS_PRIOR = 20` (line 612) enters both `readsRate` (632-634) and `readsConf` (635-637). At 30 opportunities the observed data carries only 30/50 = 60% of the weight and `conf` = 0.60, and every nudge term is multiplied by that `c` (lines 691-697). A maniac whose true VPIP is ~57% (17/30) reports a shrunk 0.452; a nit at 17% reports 0.212. The shrinkage is deliberate and documented (ARCHITECTURE.md §0 'Priors + confidence tiers'), but it means the reads model is engineered to converge slowly relative to exactly the 30-hand window the panel scored.
- THE 30-HAND BLOCK IS THE ENTIRE PRE-LABEL WARM-UP. `readLabel` returns `null` unconditionally while `rd.hands < READS_MIN_HANDS` (line 655, READS_MIN_HANDS = 30, line 613). `reads.hands` increments once per player per completed hand in `endHand` (lines 1249-1254), so every seat crosses the threshold simultaneously at hand 30 — there is no staggering and no partial reveal. Until then the Table Reads panel literally prints 'Need about 30 hands before strong labels' (lines 1483-1484), and only thin `sampleTier` hints appear from hand 12 (line 1475). Sub-gates inside `readLabel` are stricter still: `foldToCbetOpps >= 8` (line 671) requires the narrow spot at lines 1064-1068 (flop, streetBets===1, street aggressor is the preflop raiser, toCall>0), which occurs perhaps 2-4 times per bot in 30 hands, so 'overfolds c-bets' / 'calls c-bets' essentially never fires in the measured window.
- THE ADAPTATION IS APPLIED TO THE WRONG SCOPE TO READ AS MEMORY. `facingReads` is populated only when `toCall > 0` and only from the *current street aggressor's* roster reads (line 1159), and the nudge is discarded the moment the bot acts. There is no per-pair state anywhere — nothing records 'Priya has 3-bet me twice tonight'. So even where a read exists, it is an anonymous stat lookup about whoever happens to be betting, not a relationship that accumulates. `aggressorHadInitiative` (line 1160) is likewise recomputed per decision.
- THE ONE STATE THAT DOES DRIFT MONOTONICALLY ACROSS A SESSION — STACK DEPTH — IS STRUCTURALLY IGNORED. `roster[i].stack` persists (set at line 1252) and reaches the decision function as `ctx.myStack` (line 1155), but every single use of `myStack` inside `botDecide` is a `Math.min` sizing cap (lines 737, 761, 770, 793, 807, 812). Not one frequency, threshold, or bucket boundary is a function of stack depth. A bot at 40 chips and a bot at 400 play identical frequencies. Opponent stacks are not in ctx at all, and `tableSize` is passed but unused (ARCHITECTURE.md §3.1). So the most legible 'someone is having a bad night' signal on the screen has zero feedback into behavior.
- NO TIME INDEX AND NO OUTCOME HISTORY EXIST IN THE MODEL. `session.hands` / `S.handNum` are never passed into ctx (grep: lines 977, 990, 1258, 1345, 1463-1465, 1541-1551 are all display/bookkeeping). `endHand` persists exactly two facts per bot — `r.stack = p.stack` and `r.reads.hands++` (lines 1252-1253). Nothing records whether a bot won or lost the pot, was bluffed off a hand, took a beat, or showed down. The raw material for tilt or boredom is not merely unused, it is never captured. Per-hand state that could seed it (`p.aggr`, `p.inferredTier`, `p._rdPre/_rdVpip/_rdPfr`) is recreated fresh in `newHand` (line 970) and thrown away.
- PERSONA IS A FROZEN CONSTANT, NOT A TRAJECTORY. Each bot is assigned `BOT_STYLES[i-1]` by reference at `newSession` (line 936) and the five style objects (lines 831-843) are module-level `const` entries. Grep finds no assignment to `.style` and no compound mutation of `style.open/bet/fold/limp` anywhere in the file. ARCHITECTURE.md §12 states it flatly: 'styles do not change mid-session'. Every derived multiplier — `chaseMult` (787), `edgeNeed` (816), `posBet` (783), `multiway` (785) — is a pure function of style plus in-hand context, so the persona's shape at hand 30 is bit-identical to hand 1.
- WHERE ADAPTATION IS SURFACED, IT IS SURFACED AS A STAT, NOT AS A CHANGE. The only textual evidence of reading is `nudge.note` (lines 706-709), a fixed template ' Faces a loose/sticky/3-bets light aggressor.' appended to the reason. It states a classification, never a delta — nothing says 'I'm calling wider than I was because you've been betting every flop'. Meanwhile the reasons quote the *post*-nudge integer ('continues 77%, roll 0.42', line 766), so the 3-point drift from hand 1 to hand 30 is buried next to a printed random roll and reads as noise. The note is also gated by tag thresholds on shrunk rates (vpip > 0.35 or < 0.22, foldToBet > 0.55 or < 0.35, lines 699-705) that are only reachable in the last third of a 30-hand block, and only for the extreme styles.

Tell 4 has two distinct causes and they need different fixes. (a) The adaptation that exists is real but tuned an order of magnitude too small and too late: PRIOR 20 plus conf-scaling plus coefficients of 0.5/0.55/1.2 yield under one flipped decision per 30 hands, and READS_MIN_HANDS 30 puts the whole label layer outside the observed window. This is a calibration and disclosure problem, not an architecture problem — the plumbing (roster.reads, facingReads, nudge.note) is already in place and already fairness-clean. (b) Mood, tilt, boredom and stack pressure are genuinely absent at the data layer: endHand persists only stack and hands, so there is nothing to condition on. Any arc there requires new per-bot session state captured at endHand plus new ctx fields, which means re-running t3. Note the standing tension: prior shrinkage is load-bearing anti-fake-certainty design (ARCHITECTURE.md §0), so weakening it for bot decisions must not also weaken the UI's confidence tiers. Also note stacks already drift visibly on screen while changing no behavior — the cheapest available arc signal is currently a dead input.

# believability
Across BotPrize/UT2004, NPC-believability research, and poker-bot-detection literature, the consistent, actionable prescription is: bound skill with situational degradation (not uniform optimality), give bots a persistent grudge/rival state that visibly drifts over hands, vary timing with decision difficulty rather than at random, and translate internal counters into persona-voiced qualitative language instead of verbatim stats.
- BotPrize (UT2004, 2008-2012): the winning human-like bots (UT^2, MirrorBot) beat human players on 'humanness' rating (52% vs ~40%) not by being smarter but by being *worse in human ways* — imposing aim constraints so rapid/long-distance shots lose accuracy (skill bounded, not maxed), holding grudges (irrationally over-pursuing one specific rival even when it cost them), and generally being 'stubborn, overly persistent, and irrational.' Source: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317801743_Optimising_Humanness_Designing_the_Best_Human-Like_Bot_for_Unreal_Tournament_2004 , https://newatlas.com/turing-test-ut2004-botprize/24308/
- Direct actionable mapping to your bots: (1) bound each bot's skill with a persona-appropriate accuracy ceiling that degrades under specific conditions (e.g. under time pressure / after a big pot / late in session) rather than being uniformly optimal — this is the coded analogue of 'aim degrades with speed/distance.' (2) Give bots a grudge/rival mechanic: a bot that has been 3-bet or bluffed by a specific seat becomes measurably more likely to look them up or fight them again over the next N hands, even at negative EV — this alone is a strong, cheap, non-random source of visible cross-hand narrative arc (addresses judge complaint #4, 'no arc').
- Believability research consensus (multiple 2023-2026 papers, e.g. arxiv 2505.20011 'Many Challenges of Human-Like Agents', arxiv 2203.05965 'Human-Like Navigation Behavior') converges on: pixel-perfect/flawless competence reads as 'cheating AI,' and unpredictability (not randomness for its own sake, but *plausible* imperfection) correlates strongly with perceived humanness. This is a direct hit on judge complaint #2 (flawless discipline) — the fix is not more randomness in outcomes but calibrated per-persona mistake rates that are visible and occasionally costly (a loose call that loses, a fold that would've won), not just cosmetic.
- Bounded rationality / satisficing literature (ResearchGate 'From Constraints to Cognition', 2026) frames the fix formally: humans use heuristics and 'good enough' thresholds under time/attention limits rather than globally optimizing. For your frequency-policy bots this means: instead of one static frequency table per persona, let the effective threshold for a decision drift with a lightweight in-hand or in-session state variable (fatigue/boredom/frustration counter) so the same nominal policy produces different realized behavior over time — this is the mechanism for judge complaint #4 (no arc) without breaking the pure-function botDecide(ctx) constraint, as long as that state variable is itself part of ctx (session history passed in, not global/hidden state).
- Reaction-time / latency research (VR NPC realism study, arxiv 2507.10469; UX cognitive-latency writeups) finds that *fixed* low latency reads as robotic and variable latency correlated with decision difficulty reads as human — trivial folds should be fast, close river decisions slow. This is a UI-timing lever, not a policy lever, but it is the single cheapest, safest change available: tie the bot's visible 'thinking time' to a cheap proxy for decision closeness (e.g. distance of pot odds from the bot's calling threshold) rather than making it uniform or purely random.
- Poker-bot-detection industry sources (SEON, pokerbotai.com writeups on what gives bots away) independently converge on the same four tells your judges found, from the opposite direction: flat timing distributions, bet sizes precise to exact ½/⅓ pot every time in matching spots, and 'the absence of noise is itself a signal' — bots never misclick, never fat-finger a size, never show session-length variance. This validates (does not newly discover) that sizing jitter and timing jitter are load-bearing, and specifically that the *ratio precision* (always exactly 2.5x, always exactly 1/2 pot) is more damning than the raw chip amount — round-number avoidance/humanlike sizing granularity is the fix, not just 'add noise.'
- Dialogue-variety craft sources (game-writing bark design articles) converge on the mechanism for judge complaint #3 ('one voice, five hats,' recycled templates, bots quoting internal counters verbatim): professional NPC bark writers explicitly attach *situational and character-specific* detail (what the character is doing, their day-to-day context) rather than generic lines, and avoid verbatim restatement of internal stats. The actionable rule: a stated reason should reference the concrete hand/board/action texture in persona-flavored language, and internal counters (e.g. 'you've shown up 22 times') should never appear as raw numbers in dialogue — translate them to qualitative, persona-voiced judgments ('you always seem to have it,' 'I don't trust this river') that differ in phrasing per persona even when the underlying trigger is the same.

Four concrete, low-risk levers map onto the four judge-identified failures without touching botDecide's purity or the frequency-policy philosophy: (1) flawless discipline -> per-persona mistake/leak rates that are visible and sometimes costly, calibrated as a skill ceiling rather than injected pure-random noise; (2) inhuman uniformity -> sizing jitter with human-typical granularity (avoid always-exact ratios) plus reaction-time variance tied to how close the decision actually was; (3) one voice/five hats -> ban raw counter numbers from stated reasons, require persona-specific qualitative phrasing keyed off the same trigger; (4) no arc -> a grudge/rival counter and a fatigue/tilt-like state variable, both derived from session history already available in ctx (not global state), so behavior visibly drifts across 30 hands while remaining a pure function and fully reproducible/testable. None of this requires new infrastructure — it's parameter and phrasing-table work layered on the existing persona system, which fits the 'strip anything that doesn't move the humanness needle' doctrine better than the LLM-narration approach already ruled out.</implications>

# realPlayers
Real low-stakes players are defined by structural inconsistency (idiosyncratic sizing, common limping, a named calling-station archetype) plus transient, decaying emotional shifts after big pots (tilt: looser ranges, more aggression, longer sessions, loss-chasing, and a mirror-image 'winner's tilt') — not uniform discipline with occasional random noise.
- Bet sizing among recreational/live low-stakes players is idiosyncratic, not standardized: they size 'by hand class' (bigger with hands they don't want drawn out on, smaller with weak or speculative hands) rather than by pot-geometry logic, and they do not track pot size closely — meaning sizing should map loosely to hand strength/emotional state with noise, never a fixed BB multiple or clean ladder (pokerskill.com sizing-tell glossary; crushlivepoker.com bet-sizing-patterns; blackrain79 bet-sizing-tricks).
- A validated sizing tell requires 'a few repeats of the non-baseline size in the same spot category' before it means anything — i.e. real players are inconsistent hand-to-hand, and a tell only emerges as a statistical lean over a sample, not a rule that fires every time (crushlivepoker.com/articles/weak-bet-sizing-tells).
- Limping is common and often correct in $1-2/$2-5 live low-stakes pools: 'low isolation frequencies, limited three-betting, and a tendency toward multiway flops regardless of preflop sizing' — small pairs/suited connectors get limped for implied odds because callers behind rarely punish it. A 30-hand bot block with zero limps and pure raise-or-fold is a structural tell of non-humanness (888poker limping article; pokertube limping article).
- Calling stations are a named, common low-stakes archetype defined by a wide VPIP-PFR gap (regular ~28/21; calling station gap of 15+, extreme example ~50/7/0.6AF/45WTS) — they call too wide preflop AND postflop, hate folding weak draws, and go to showdown far more often. This is a distinct, stable population segment, not universal looseness — meaning bot variety should include at least one persona whose 'bad calls' are frequent and specific (weak draws, wide preflop calls) rather than sprinkling occasional randomness across all personas equally (hand2note, pokercode, americascardroom calling-station glossary pages).
- Tilt (Wikipedia + PMC7400001 clinical study, n=291) has concrete, replicable signatures: (1) escalating aggression away from baseline strategy, (2) loosened hand selection — 'playing junk hands with lower win chance,' (3) a loss-chasing motivation specifically to recover money just lost, (4) extended session length while tilted (60% of studied sessions >2hrs, 26% >4hrs — i.e. tilted players don't quit, they keep firing), and (5) 'winner's tilt' — overconfidence-driven loose/aggressive play after a big win, not just after bad beats. This gives a testable spec: a tilt state should transiently raise looseness+aggression+bluff-frequency for a few hands after a big pot lost (or won), then decay back to baseline — not a permanent shift.
- A separate tracker-based framing (rakeback.com HUD article) operationalizes tilt as measurable via post-loss VPIP shift versus baseline (worked example: baseline VPIP 0.40 -> post-loss-window VPIP 0.52), giving a concrete, cite-able magnitude (roughly +0.10-0.12 VPIP, i.e. a meaningfully looser but not unrecognizable range) for however many hands the tilt window covers — useful as a calibration anchor rather than an arbitrary +20% loosening constant.

For the bot policies: (1) bet sizing per persona should be a noisy function of hand-strength-class plus persona identity, never a fixed multiplier — the 'consistency across repeats' finding means occasional off-baseline sizes are fine and expected, they just shouldn't happen every single hand identically. (2) Reintroduce limping as a first-class preflop action for looser personas (calling-station, maniac, even solid-regular occasionally) with speculative hands, not just raise/fold. (3) Give at least one persona (calling-station) a stable wide-VPIP/narrow-PFR profile with genuine bad calls on weak draws, rather than spreading small noise everywhere. (4) Implement tilt/heater as a decaying multi-hand state triggered by big pot outcomes (won or lost) that transiently loosens VPIP by roughly +0.10 and raises aggression/bluff frequency for a handful of hands, then reverts — this directly answers judges' 'no arc' and 'flawless discipline' complaints while staying a pure, seedable function of ctx (recent pot-history-derived state passed into ctx, not global mutable state) so fairness/determinism constraints hold. Stated reasons for tilt-driven or loose plays must stay truthful to the actual hand/action, e.g. describing the wide call honestly rather than fabricating a hand-strength justification.

# priorArt
Every "humanizing" trick that works commercially reduces to five deterministic-friendly techniques — seeded noise on numeric outputs, a session-persistent mood/tilt state machine, a tagged template grammar for varied text, per-opponent hidden trait vectors that bias (not replace) the existing frequency tables, and small-sample-driven inconsistency — all of which slot into botDecide's existing ctx-in/decision-out contract without adding hidden state or breaking purity.
- Seeded parameter noise ("jitter") is the standard game-dev fix for mechanical uniformity: Perlin/simplex noise modulated onto a numeric output (position, rotation, timing) produces organic-feeling variation while staying fully deterministic and controllable via frequency/amplitude — directly transferable to bet sizing (jitter the raise-to amount ±X% seeded by hand/seat/street) instead of the current exact-7-chip ladder. Source: Perlin Noise in Procedural Animation (palospublishing.com), GameDev.net thread on Perlin noise for subtle random motion.
- Mixed-strategy randomization is poker's own native answer to 'flawless discipline': GTO solvers deliberately mix actions at fixed frequencies (e.g. bet a hand 60%, check it 40%) specifically so no single hand-state maps to one deterministic action — this is exactly the 'frequencies not rules' philosophy ARCHITECTURE.md already commits to, and the fix for INHUMAN UNIFORMITY / FLAWLESS DISCIPLINE is to push existing frequency tables toward genuinely mixed outputs at hand-level granularity (not just style-tier granularity) rather than adding new logic. Source: GTO Wizard 'How Solvers Work', Red Chip Poker 'The World of GTO Poker'.
- Bethesda's Radiant AI (Oblivion) and The Sims both simulate personality through a small persistent state vector (needs/moodlets) that decays and gets nudged by events, then biases otherwise-generic behavior selection — not through per-NPC bespoke scripts. The Sims explicitly randomizes moodlet *strength* (not just occurrence) so the same trigger doesn't produce identical results twice. This is the direct model for a 'tilt/mood' scalar per bot that persists across hands in a session and nudges (not overrides) the existing frequency policy — satisfying NO ARC without inventing a second decision system. Sources: paavohtl's blog 'What was Radiant AI, anyway?', Carl's Sims 4 Guide 'How Emotions Work', UESP Oblivion:Radiant.
- Crusader Kings/Football Manager encode personality as a fixed hidden trait vector per agent (CK3 personality traits bias AI decision weighting; FM's Consistency attribute explicitly controls how often a player deviates from their average performance band) rather than as behavior rules — this is the 'ONE VOICE, FIVE HATS' fix: give each bot a small hidden trait vector (e.g. bluff-frequency bias, sizing-tell bias, tilt-susceptibility, patience) that perturbs the shared frequency tables and shared reason-template selection differently per seat, so the same game state produces persona-distinct outputs without new per-bot code paths. Sources: footballmanagerblog.org 'Ultimate Guide to Hidden Attributes', CK2/CK3 Traits wiki (paradoxwikis.com).
- Tracery (GalaxyKate, used for the Cheap Bots Done Quick generative-Twitter-bot ecosystem) is the proven lightweight pattern for template text variation without an LLM: tag dialogue fragments by register/context, expand via a grammar with saved-and-reused symbols, and suppress recently-used exact sequences so the same trigger doesn't emit the same string twice in a session. This maps directly onto the 'recycled reason templates' problem — a small per-persona phrase-bank keyed by (action, hand-strength-bucket, mood-state) with a 'no repeat within N hands' guard would fix ONE VOICE FIVE HATS while keeping every emitted reason a truthful restatement of the actual ctx-derived decision (never generated free text). Source: tracery.io, Programming Historian 'Intro to Twitterbots with Tracery', GalaxyKate/Compton et al. 'Tracery: An Author-Focused Generative Text Tool'.
- Left 4 Dead's AI Director is the clean precedent for session-level 'arc' without per-encounter scripting: it tracks a running intensity/fatigue metric across the whole play session and uses that single accumulator to modulate pacing (spawn density, item availability) rather than scripting each encounter — the poker analogue is a single running 'session state' (variance run, recent win/loss streak, hands since last big pot) that feeds into each bot's mood/tilt nudge, giving a visible arc across 30 hands from one small piece of shared state rather than five independent bot brains. Source: Left 4 Dead Wiki 'The Director', GameSpot retrospective on L4D design.
- A recurring caveat across every source: none of these systems (Radiant AI, Sims moodlets, GTO mixed strategies, CK3 traits) generate free text or free decisions — they always constrain randomness/state to bias a fixed, bounded action/output space. This matches the project's hard constraint that stated reasons must stay truthful and botDecide must stay a pure ctx function: mood/trait/noise values must be derived inside ctx-available inputs (or passed in as new explicit ctx fields, e.g. a seeded RNG stream and a persistent-but-still-public trait/mood record), never smuggled in as hidden global state, to keep the existing fairness proof and t3.js traps intact.

The concrete, right-sized path: (1) add a seeded jitter function wrapping bet/raise sizing so opens/raises vary within a tight band instead of exact chip counts — cheap, high-leverage against INHUMAN UNIFORMITY; (2) extend BOT_STYLES with a small hidden per-seat trait vector (bluff bias, tilt susceptibility, patience) that nudges existing frequency tables, addressing ONE VOICE FIVE HATS without new decision branches; (3) add one session-scoped mood/tilt scalar per seat, updated each hand from public outcomes (win/loss streak, big pot lost) and passed into ctx or derived from ctx-visible history, to produce NO ARC drift and occasional loose/tilted deviations for FLAWLESS DISCIPLINE; (4) replace single-string reason templates with a small Tracery-style tagged phrase bank per persona keyed on (action, hand bucket, mood) with a no-repeat-within-N-hands guard, keeping every phrase a truthful restatement of the real decision. All four are additive to the existing ctx-in/decision-out contract, keep botDecide pure and testable, and were each independently proven (not merely theorized) in shipped, well-documented systems. None require LLMs or free-text generation, respecting the already-ruled-out constraint.</implications>
