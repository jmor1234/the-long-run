# Design: modelFirst

## Diagnosis
The bots fail as people because the code contains no model of a person. A person at a table is three things: (a) a policy whose parameters are individually noisy — my sizing, my calling threshold, my sloppiness are *mine* and not my archetype's; (b) an emotional state that recent public history pushes around and that decays back — tonight I lost a big pot to Priya and for the next six hands I am looser and I want her specifically; (c) a voice that expresses (a) and (b) in first person. The current file has exactly one of the three, and only in degenerate form: five shared archetype objects, frozen, with four scalar dials, zero per-seat variance, zero session state beyond a stack number nothing reads, and reason strings welded to control flow so phrasing count equals branch count. Every one of the four judge tells is a downstream symptom of that single absence, which is why they converged: uniformity is "no per-seat noise on parameters, and sizing isn't even a parameter"; flawless discipline is "no emotional state, and the highest-frequency decision is a step function"; one voice is "no voice layer at all"; no arc is "no state that can drift." The fix is therefore not four patches but one model — per-seat trait DNA × a per-seat drifting mood × a rendering voice — plus two plumbing repairs (a real sizing function, a soft decision boundary) without which traits and mood have no channel through which to become visible.

## Mechanisms
### Seat DNA — per-seat trait vector drawn once per session [moderate]
Replace the frozen `BOT_STYLES[i-1]` reference at poker-trainer.html:936 with `style: makeSeat(BOT_STYLES[i-1], Math.random)`. `makeSeat(arch, rnd)` returns a frozen object: the archetype's `open/bet/fold/limp/tag/blurb` each perturbed by ±12% multiplicative jitter, plus NEW named dials that today do not exist anywhere: `sizeBase` (preflop open in BB — archetype means nit 2.5, solid 3.0, maniac 3.6, selective 2.8, station 2.6, realized ×(0.85+0.3·r) → 2.1–4.2 BB), `sizeNoise` (per-decision jitter amplitude 0.06–0.18: some people are metronomic, some erratic), `sizeTell` (0–0.35, signed hand-strength→size correlation — the documented recreational leak of sizing by hand class; station/nit high, solid ≈0.05), `oddSize` (0.03–0.10 chance of an overbet/underbet outlier), `edge` (required-equity offset: nit +0.06 … station −0.06, replacing the 1.3-point `edgeNeed` spread at line 816), `temp` (decision temperature 0.03–0.09, see mechanism 3), `chase` (draw-equity multiplier 0.8–1.6, replacing the derived `chaseMult` at 787), `tilt` (mood susceptibility 0.3–1.4), `patience` (boredom susceptibility 0.3–1.4), `voice` (0–2, sub-flavor inside the persona's phrase bank). Traits are fixed for the session — persona is who you are; mood is how you are tonight — so ARCHITECTURE.md §12's 'styles do not change mid-session' survives intact. Rides into `botDecide` through the EXISTING `ctx.style` field (passed whole at line 1161), so this mechanism alone needs no ctx change. Drawn with `Math.random()` inside `newSession`, which the exp harness already routes to the seeded `session` stream, so it is reproducible per seed.

Closes: Tells 1 and 3. It is the only thing that makes two bots of the same persona, or the same bot across seeds, behave and sound differently, and it is what turns sizing from a constant into a per-person parameter. It also widens persona separation from 1.3 points of required equity to a real spread (edge + temp + chase).

### Mood — one drifting per-seat scalar built from public outcomes, plus a per-rival heat [moderate]
Add `r.self = {mood:0, dry:0, rivals:{}, recent:[]}` to each roster entry at newSession. Update once per hand inside `endHand` (after the pot award loop at 1249-1255, where `p.stack` is final and every input is public): `const bb=(p.stack-stackAtHandStart)/BB; const impact=clamp(bb/25,-1,1); r.self.mood = clamp(0.78*r.self.mood + impact*(bb<0?1.0:0.6)*t.tilt, -1, 1);` — decay 0.78 gives a ~3-hand half-life, so a 40bb loss pins a tilt-prone seat near −1 and it takes 6–8 hands to return: several visible episodes per 30-hand block, and mood autocorrelation ≈0.78, which is exactly what 'an arc' means. `dry` increments on hands the bot never put money in voluntarily, resets when it plays; `bore = clamp((dry-6)/10,0,1)*t.patience`. `rivals[seat]` decays ×0.85/hand and gains +1 when that seat aggressed into a pot this bot contested and took it — the BotPrize grudge, one scalar, scoped at the call site to the current aggressor only. ctx gains ONE new field, built in step() at 1153-1162 from roster data only: `self:{mood, bore, stackBB:p.stack/BB, grudge:(agg?me.self.rivals[agg.seat]||0:0), hands:me.reads.hands, recent:me.self.recent}`. No cards, no S, no hole-card-derived quantity — but it IS a ctx change, so t3.js is mandatory (per §3.1) and its ctx-leak scan should be extended to walk nested objects. Inside botDecide, mood collapses immediately into exactly two scalars used everywhere, which is what keeps this coherent rather than sprawling: `looseAdd = clamp(0.14*max(0,-mood) + 0.09*max(0,mood) + 0.10*bore + 0.11*min(1,grudge/3), 0, 0.30)` (loss-chasing weighted above winner's tilt, both real per the tilt literature; the 0.10–0.14 ceiling is the research-anchored +10–12pp VPIP shift, not an arbitrary constant) and `aggMult = clamp(1 + 0.30*max(0,-mood)*t.tilt + 0.15*max(0,mood) + 0.10*bore + 0.12*min(1,grudge/3) + (stackBB<40?0.12:stackBB>120?0.05:0), 0.75, 1.45)`. Applied at exactly six existing sites: `thr *= (1+1.6*looseAdd)` (731), `limpFreq += 0.25*looseAdd` (721/742), `defendCall`/`spewCall` `*= (1+2*looseAdd)` (756-757), postflop bet frequencies `*= aggMult` (790-791, 806, 811), `edgeNeed` (816) becomes `t.edge*(1-1.8*looseAdd)` with the floor lowered from +0.005 to −0.05 so a genuinely bad call is representable, and the soft-boundary temperature in mechanism 3. Note `stackBB`: the one quantity that already drifts visibly on screen and currently feeds nothing.

Closes: Tells 4 and 2, from one mechanism. Arc, because mood is autocorrelated over the exact 30-hand window judges watch, unlike `facingNudge` whose realized effect is <1 flipped decision per block. Discipline, because bad calls and loose opens now arrive in *clusters* tied to a visible cause (that bot just lost a big pot to that player) rather than as uniform noise — which is what 'having a bad night' looks like and what pure randomness cannot imitate.

### Soft decision boundary + independent rolls [small]
Two edits inside botDecide. (a) Replace the deterministic step at lines 817-822 — the highest-frequency decision at the table — with a logistic mix: `const margin = effective - need - edgeNeed; const pCall = 1/(1+Math.exp(-margin/temp));` where `temp = t.temp*(1+0.6*looseAdd)`, then roll. Same one line in, one line out; it simultaneously makes bad calls AND bad folds possible, makes closeness continuous, differentiates personas by sloppiness (temperature) and not only by threshold, and yields a truthful closeness value for the voice layer and the timing rider. Apply the same treatment to the two unconditional preflop returns: line 749's fold is now reachable by the widened limp/curiosity band, and line 772's terminal fold gains a small `curiosity` frequency from `looseAdd` for suited/connected junk. (b) The function currently draws ONE `Math.random()` (line 725) and reuses it for every comparison; draw independent rolls for branch selection, sizing jitter, and the soft call, removing the correlation artifact where a high roll deterministically co-fires several branches. Update the `n!==5` Math.random-site assertion in exp/exp-harness.js:44 to the new count — that guard exists precisely to force this re-audit; determinism is preserved automatically because every new draw goes through `Math.random()` and lands on the per-decision seeded stream.

Closes: Tell 2 directly and structurally: 'zero loose calls in 27 hands' is currently a theorem about the code, not a tuning outcome. It also un-breaks ARCHITECTURE §3.4 honestly — the doc concedes 'some postflop call/fold branches are threshold-based'; in fact all of them were.

### One human sizing function, and removal of both collapse points [moderate]
Add `function humanSize(rawChips, t, r, myStack)` in the BOT POLICY section: jitter by `±t.sizeNoise`, apply granularity (`<12 → nearest 1, <40 → nearest 5, else nearest 10` — people bet round-ish numbers, and industry bot-detection sources say ratio precision is more damning than the amount), clamp to `[2, myStack]`. Every sizing site routes through it. Preflop open (line 737): delete `Math.max(6, …)` — the floor that makes every open exactly 6 — and compute `raw = BB*(t.sizeBase + limpers + tell)` where `limpers=(pot-3)/2` (public) and `tell = t.sizeTell*(pct<8 ? +0.6 : pct>25 ? -0.4 : 0)` in BB. Realized opens span roughly 4–9 chips with a per-seat centre and a per-seat readable lean. 3-bets (761, 770): `raw = pot + 1.6*toCall`, which is ~15–19 chips where the current `round(pot*1.0)`=9 falls below the legal minimum of 10 and gets silently overwritten by `applyAction`'s clamp at line 1099 — that clamp stops being the de facto sizing policy and goes back to being a legality backstop. Postflop (780-781, 793, 807, 812): per-bucket base fractions (strong 0.75, good 0.60, marginal 0.40, semi-bluff 0.55) × a street term (turn 1.05, river 1.10) × the `sizeTell` lean, × an `oddSize`-gated outlier (×1.5 or ×0.35, ~6% of bets), then humanSize. Also promote `currentBet` and `minRaise` into the shipped ctx at 1153-1162 (both are public and already on screen): sizing becomes legality-aware, deliberate min-raises become a *choice* some seats make rather than an artifact, and exp-harness's exp-only ctx extension at line ~38 can be deleted — a net simplification.

Closes: Tell 1 entirely. 'Every open is exactly 7 chips' and 'robotic min-raise ladders' are two separate mechanical collapses (the `Math.max(6,…)` floor; policy sizes falling under the legal minimum). This removes both and gives sizing a per-seat identity, so it also carries persona — the maniac and the nit stop betting the identical number of chips into the identical pot.

### Voice layer — structured intent out of botDecide, persona phrase bank renders it [moderate]
botDecide stops returning prose. Each `return` carries `say:{intent, slots}` — e.g. `{intent:'callDown', slots:{hand, tex, odds:{toCall,pot}, read, moodTag}}` — and a new section `BOT VOICE`, placed immediately above BOT POLICY, holds `phrase(say, style, recent, r)`: a pure function selecting from `BANK[persona][intent]`, an array of ≥6 templates per (persona, intent), skipping any template id present in `ctx.self.recent` (a 6-deep ring maintained in roster; step() writes back `me.self.recent` from the returned `tid` after applyAction, keeping phrase() pure). Templates are slot-filling only — no free generation — so every emitted sentence is a rendering of the decision that was actually made. Concrete register rules, all mechanically testable: first person ('I', 'my' — currently 0 of 753 reasons contain either); chips not internal percentages ('9 to call into 26', which is real math and real table-speak, so curriculum value survives); board texture from the actual board ('paired', 'two-tone', 'ace-high', 'ragged' — currently 1 of 753 reasons mentions any board feature); zero RNG values, zero branch names ('flat-call branch', 'the slow branch'), zero raw read counters. Reads are translated through the ALREADY-COMPUTED `sampleTier`/`s.conf` into three registers — `unknown` says nothing about the opponent, `lean` hedges ('feels like he's been at this a lot'), `solid` commits ('he never folds') — which kills the current defect where a 5-opportunity read and a 200-opportunity read speak with identical certainty. `facingNudge`'s frozen `note` string (lines 706-709) is deleted and replaced by a structured read tag consumed by the bank. Mood and grudge get their own truthful slots ('that last pot stung and I'm not in a folding mood' is emitted only when mood is actually negative and the bot is actually calling wider). Machine detail does not vanish, it moves: the numeric frequency/roll view goes behind an `opts.verbose` dev toggle and stays in the dossier/equity strip, which are already the app's machine-signal surfaces.

Closes: Tell 3 entirely, and it is the only mechanism that can address it, because phrasing count is currently pinned to branch count by construction. It also converts mechanisms 1, 2 and 4 from invisible internals into things a judge and a learner can actually perceive — traits and mood that nobody narrates read as noise.

### Multiway and stack-depth guardrails [small]
Rename the misnamed `multiway` at line 785 (it keys off `streetBets`, not opponents) and add the genuinely public `liveOpps` (count of unfolded opponents) to ctx. Use it in two places: bucket boundaries shift up with field size (`strong` at `0.82 + 0.03*(liveOpps-1)`), and bluff/semi-bluff frequencies scale `×(1 - 0.10*(liveOpps-1))`. This is the safety valve for mechanism 2 — without it, mood-driven loosening produces absurd five-way spew — and it is also just correct: every bot currently evaluates its hand against exactly one random opponent no matter how many are live.

Closes: None of the four directly; it is the bound that keeps the tilt/boredom loosening plausible rather than cartoonish, and it prevents the humanness work from degrading the trainer into 'just bet, they all call.'

### Think-time scaled by decision closeness [trivial]
botDecide returns `think` (0–1) from the quantity it already computes — `|margin|/temp` for the soft call, `|roll − freq|` elsewhere. `step()`'s fixed `620` at line 1166 becomes `260 + 900*think + 200*Math.random()`: trivial folds snap, close river calls hang. Explicitly NOT counted toward the blind-panel measurement — transcripts cannot show timing — but the panel is a proxy and the live learner is the goal, and the fixed-latency tell is well documented in the bot-detection literature.

Closes: None as measured; it is the cheapest available improvement to the real player's experience and costs three lines.

## Refused
- LLM narration or LLM decisions in any form — pre-registered and settled (exp/PLAN.md): 3–3.5/10 and hallucinated reasons, disqualifying when reasons are the curriculum.
- A Tracery-style recursive grammar engine. Flat template arrays with typed slots plus a no-repeat ring produce all the variety measurable at ~20 reasons per hand; a grammar adds a nondeterminism surface and an authoring burden for zero incremental judge-visible effect.
- Perlin/simplex noise for sizing jitter. At integer chip granularity with a ±12% band, uniform jitter is statistically indistinguishable from coherent noise. The cited game-dev precedent is about smooth motion, not scalar amounts.
- A full per-pair relationship matrix (who 3-bet whom, when, with what). One decayed scalar per rival is enough to produce visible cross-hand narrative; the matrix is state nobody reads.
- Bot memory of its own actions earlier in the hand or street. It needs new ctx fields, another t3 cycle, and buys much less than mood does.
- Weakening READS_PRIOR or READS_MIN_HANDS to force the reads channel to show inside 30 hands. That prior is load-bearing anti-fake-certainty design (§0), and mechanism 2 delivers the arc from a channel that does not have to lie about sample size. The confidence-tiered voice registers surface the reads honestly instead.
- Any mid-session mutation of the style object. Traits are identity; drift lives in mood. Keeping them separate is what preserves the §12 invariant and keeps the model explicable.
- Per-persona bespoke decision branches. Every persona difference is a parameter in the trait vector or a bank index — no new control flow, per the frequencies-not-rules doctrine.
- Browser persistence of mood or traits across sessions (§0: no localStorage).

## Risks
- Loosening the bots could make them exploitable and teach the wrong habit — the exact failure mode ARCHITECTURE §3.4 warns about. Mitigations: looseAdd hard-capped at 0.30, mood mean-reverting (0.78 decay) so session-average VPIP moves only a few points, the multiway guardrail, and a mandatory exploit probe (the historical always-re-raise and maniac-hero policies must still lose heavily).
- ctx grows by `self`, `liveOpps`, `currentBet`, `minRaise`. t3.js is mandatory and its runtime ctx-leak scan must be extended to walk nested objects, since today it only inspects top-level values for card-shaped arrays.
- phrase() and the phrase bank sit OUTSIDE t3's static scan window (which slices from `function botDecide` to the GAME STATE banner). Extend the slice to cover the BOT VOICE section, or the fairness ban list silently stops covering code that runs inside a bot decision.
- The voice layer is the largest surface for regression into fiction. Mitigated by slot-only templates plus a `needs:[...]` precondition tag per template validated at emit time in tests (a draw-claiming line cannot fire without a real draw) — but this is the one place where a careless future edit can violate the hardest constraint in the project.
- Mood derives from stack deltas at endHand; the uncalled-bet refund path (§3.3) must not read as a win. Measure net around the full award loop so refunds net to zero.
- New randomness must go through Math.random() only. Date.now, crypto, or a private RNG would silently break the exp harness's seeded replay and every reproducibility claim.
- Bank size is the failure mode for tell 3 specifically: fewer than ~6 variants per (persona, intent) and the recycled-template complaint returns in a new costume. This is authoring volume, roughly 120 lines of data, and it must not be trimmed.
- File grows to ~1850 lines, mostly phrase data. Acceptable, but ARCHITECTURE §2's section map and §3.1's ctx list must be updated in the same increment or the cold-start doc goes stale.

## Verification
"Regression floor first: `bash run-all.sh` clean, and `node t3.js` is mandatory and non-negotiable because ctx changed (§3.1). Then two new deterministic audits, both seeded, both free, each with an oracle independent of the policy code. `t7.js` (voice): over ~2000 seeded hands assert no reason contains `roll`, a branch name, an unresolved `{`, or a raw counter; distinct surface skeletons ≥120 per 750 reasons (baseline 41) with top-3 share <15% (baseline 53%); pairwise persona word-set Jaccard <0.6; and a truth table — every emitted intent matches the returned action, and every template tagged `needs:['draw'|'pairedBoard'|…]` is validated against a drawInfo/board recomputed from the ctx, so a fabricated claim fails the build. `t8.js` (texture): ≥6 distinct preflop open sizes; share of raises landing exactly on the legal minimum <25% (currently ~100% of 3-bets); tightest-to-loosest per-persona VPIP spread ≥15pp (currently ~0 postflop); the same (strength, pot-odds) bucket produces both calls and folds; overall bad-call rate (called with margin<0) between 4% and 15%; mood lag-1 autocorrelation >0.4. Plus an exploitability probe reusing the historical hero policies — always-re-raise and maniac must still lose badly, which is the guard on §3.4. The needle itself is measured by the pipeline that produced the 1–1.5 score: run the paired blind panel from `exp/` (run-feel.js + prompt.js + the block/key protocol), new coded arm against the current coded arm on identical seeds so cards are shared and only decisions and reasons differ, 16 independent judges, same rubric plus the four tells as named sub-items. Pre-register the target before looking: mean humanness ≥5/10, and a strict majority of paired blocks where the new arm is marked human and its paired old block mechanical. Also update exp-harness.js's `Math.random()` site-count assertion and delete its now-redundant exp-only ctx extension; a failure there is a signal to re-audit stream assignment, not a number to bump blindly. Finally, walk the real app: mood, grudge and sizing are supposed to be legible to a person watching the table, and only playing it proves the reasons still teach."
